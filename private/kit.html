PROMPT 1 · STRUCTUREIn ChatGPT

Turn your idea into a Build Brief

Fill in only the two brackets at the top. Don't answer the questions yourself: ChatGPT asks them one at a time, then writes the brief.

I want to build: [your one sentence].
It's for: [who will use it].

Ask me up to 8 short questions, one at a time, to understand what it must do. Then write a Build Brief with these headings:

1. What it is (one sentence)
2. Who it's for
3. The one job it does
4. Must have (no more than 5 things)

A build nobody can open isn't finished. Netlify gives your page a real link you can send to anyone, and Claude can publish to it for you.

PROMPT 8 · UPDATEIn Claude

Update the live site

After every Refine you're happy with. The link stays the same, so anyone you've sent it to sees the new version when they refresh.

Update my Netlify site [site name or link] with the latest version of this page.
Keep the same link. Tell me what changed.

ACheck it properly

  1. Open the link on your phone in a private tab.
  2. Send it to one person and watch them use it without help.
  3. Anything they get stuck on is your next Prompt 3.

BNo connector? Drag and drop

  1. Ask Claude: "Give me this page as one HTML file called index.html."
  2. Save it into a folder on your computer.
  3. Go to app.netlify.com/drop and drag the folder in.
WHEN YOU'RE READY

Your own domain

Buy the domain from any registrar (Namecheap, Cloudflare, Names.co.uk and so on). In Netlify, open your site, then Domain management, then Add a domain, and follow the DNS steps it shows. Netlify adds HTTPS for you. If the DNS step confuses you, screenshot it and ask Claude what to enter at your registrar.

Netlify's free plan has a monthly usage allowance. A small site fits comfortably. If a site ever goes over, Netlify tells you, and you can upgrade or wait for the next month.

Create

Motion graphics library

17 styles, ready to run. Each prompt sets the style with numbers, not adjectives: colours, timing, easing and what to avoid. Swap the brackets for your brand and message.

ADD TO ANY MOTION PROMPT

Five rules that lift every result

  • Numbers, not adjectives"#9040FF, 24 frames, 80px margins" beats "bold and modern".
  • A hook in the first secondThe first frame has to make someone stop scrolling.
  • Text needs timeAbout 0.3 seconds per word on screen, never less than 1 second, inside a 5% safe margin.
  • Hold the last frameThe final frame, usually the logo, stays still for about 1 second.
  • Say what to avoidEvery prompt below ends with an Avoid line. Keep it.
Before you build it, show me a storyboard: one line per shot with its timing. Wait for my OK.
End on a logo reveal: the logo forms from the shapes used in the piece, then holds for 1 second on [background colour].
Graphic and typeSwiss kinetic type · Shape morph · Vertical social captions
SWISS GRID AND KINETIC TYPE

Best for: brand identity, event openers, conference titles

Make a [length, e.g. 15]-second motion graphic in a Swiss grid and kinetic typography style for [brand or event].
Lines, in order: [3 to 5 short lines of text].
Format: [1920x1080 / 1080x1920 / 1080x1080], 60fps.
Style: a strict 12-column grid with 80px margins. Black, off-white, and one signal colour, [hex], on one element per shot only. One heavy grotesque typeface. Every move snaps on one easing curve, cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.2, 1). Shots change with hard wipes along the grid lines.
Avoid: fades, gradients, drop shadows, a second accent colour, centred layouts.
SHAPE MORPH AND LIQUID

Best for: music visuals, brand stories, moving between topics

Make a [length]-second shape morph animation for [brand] that tells this in 4 beats: [beat 1, beat 2, beat 3, beat 4].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: one continuous shot with no cuts. Each beat is a single bold shape that melts into the next over 18 to 30 frames. Hold each shape for at least 1 second so it reads. Palette: [3 hex colours], flat fills, smooth organic edges. The final shape becomes the logo.
Avoid: hard cuts, outlines, more than one shape moving at a time, text over the morphs.
VERTICAL SOCIAL CAPTIONS

Best for: Reels, Shorts, TikTok, founder clips

Make a [length, e.g. 20]-second vertical social video, 1080x1920, for [brand or person].
Script, as spoken: [paste the lines].
Style: big captions, 1 to 4 words at a time, timed to the script, kept inside the middle 80% of the frame so platform buttons don't cover them. One accent word per caption in [hex]. Background: [a colour, a slow gradient, or "my attached image"]. Captions pop in over 4 frames with a small overshoot.
Avoid: full sentences on screen, text in the bottom 20%, more than two colours of text.
Explain and sellFlat vector explainer · Data story · Dark tech keynote · Living UI
FLAT VECTOR EXPLAINER

Best for: product promos, onboarding, how it works

Make a [length, e.g. 30]-second flat vector explainer for [product].
The story in 4 steps: [the problem, what you do, how it works, the result].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: flat illustration with 4 to 5 colours, [hex codes], no gradients. Elements arrive with a small overshoot and settle within 12 frames. Groups of items stagger by 2 to 4 frames. Simple friendly characters with no facial detail.
Avoid: gradients, 3D, realistic textures, more than 7 words on screen at once.
DATA STORY

Best for: reports, impact numbers, investor updates

Make a [length]-second data story for [organisation] using only these numbers: [paste each figure with its source].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: one chart per screen. Bars and lines grow over 20 frames, then hold. Axis labels at least 28px. One highlight colour, [hex], for the number that matters, everything else in greys. Each screen ends on one plain sentence about what the number means.
Avoid: made-up numbers, 3D charts, pie charts with more than 4 slices, decorative icons.
DARK TECH KEYNOTE

Best for: product launches, feature reveals, AI and software

Make a [length, e.g. 15]-second launch film in a dark tech keynote style for [product].
The reveal: [the headline feature], and the one number that proves it: [the number].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: near-black background, soft studio light, one accent colour, [hex]. Slow, confident camera moves of 30 to 45 frames. The key number appears huge, about 30% of the frame height. Clean sans serif, generous space.
Avoid: lens flares, fast cuts, busy backgrounds, more than one accent colour.

Part 3 · Full control in the Editor

Whatever Claude or the Brand tool makes, the Editor is where your taste takes over. It works the way Photoshop does.

  • Layers, Properties and HistoryEvery element is its own layer. Undo anything from History.
  • TypeDouble-click the canvas to type. Use Google Fonts or font files from your computer.
  • Remove backgroundCreates a mask, so nothing is lost and you can paint it back.
  • 58 filters, as layersStack them, mask them and fade them. The same effects are one click away at voidcanvas.app/effects.
  • 12 format presetsFrom Instagram post to A4 flyer. Download all as a ZIP.
  • Import your filesPSD keeps layers, groups and blend modes. A PDF comes in with each page as a layer.
  • ExportPNG, JPG or WebP up to 3x, or a print PDF at 300 dpi with bleed and crop marks.
  • Shortcuts you knowV move, T type, M marquee, B brush, E eraser.

Claude for business

The same method works on the rest of your work: brief it, direct it, judge it. These prompts cover finding clients, winning the work, running it and growing. Turn on web search in Claude for any prompt that says so.

Set up once

1

One Project per client

In claude.ai, open Projects and make one per client. Add their brief, brand files and past emails as project knowledge, and write instructions like "British English. Emails under 150 words." Every chat there starts with that context. Free plans get up to five projects.

2

Connect Gmail, Calendar and Drive

In a chat, click +, then Connectors. Claude can then search and draft emails, find a time in your calendar and pull documents from Drive. It asks before it sends anything.

3

Teach it your voice

Paste three emails you've sent and say: "Write like this from now on." Put that in the project instructions so you only do it once.

The rules that keep you safeClaude drafts, you send. Check every name, number and date before it leaves you. Don't paste a client's confidential data or passwords unless they've agreed. For contracts, tax or legal questions, use Claude to understand the document, then check with a professional.

Find clients

PROSPECT RESEARCHClaude, web search on

Walk into every call prepared

Research [company name] ([website]) before my call with [person, role] on [date].
Use web search and list your sources.
Give me:
1. What they do, who they sell to, and roughly how big they are.
2. Anything they've announced in the last 6 months.
3. Three problems they probably have that I can help with. I offer [your service].
4. Five questions to ask on the call.
5. One opening line that shows I did my homework.
If you can't find something, say so. Don't guess.
LEAD LISTClaude, web search on

Build a list of organisations worth contacting

Works across markets, for example companies in Ghana, Kenya and Nigeria. Every row comes with a source you can check.

Help me build a list of [type of organisation, e.g. logistics companies in Ghana and Kenya with 50 to 500 staff] that might need [your service].
Use web search. For each one give: name, website, country, what they do in one line, why they might need this, and the public source you used.
Only include organisations you can verify with a source. Start with 15. Put it in a table I can paste into a spreadsheet.
Don't invent people's names or email addresses.
OUTREACHIn Claude

A three-email sequence that gets replies

Write a 3-email outreach sequence to [role] at [company].
What I offer: [one sentence].
Why them: [the specific reason, e.g. something from the research].
Proof: [one result you've delivered, with a number if you have it].
Rules: under 90 words each, plain English, one clear ask per email, no "I hope this finds you well", no exclamation marks.
Email 1: first contact. Email 2: 4 days later, adds one new useful point. Email 3: 7 days later, short and easy to say no to.
Give each one a subject line.

Use only what's in my notes. Where something is missing, write [ASK CLIENT] instead of guessing.
My rates and constraints: [your day rate or price range, your availability].

4. Anything that was left unclear.

Fixes and FAQ

The problems people hit most, including the ones we fixed together on Saturday.

  • Pictures from a website don't load in your buildClaude can only reach sites you allow. In Claude, open Settings, then Capabilities, and add the image site's domain to the allowed domains list (for property listings that was media.onthemarket.com). Then ask Claude to try again.
  • Your build answers like a chat, but you wanted a websiteSay: "Turn this into a website: one page that shows the results as cards, with a filter at the top and a link on each card. Keep the same data source."
  • Claude shows code but no pageCheck that code execution and file creation are on in Claude's settings. Then ask: "Show this as a page I can preview."
  • Claude changed things you didn't ask forSay: "Undo that. Go back to the previous version and change only [the one thing]."
  • Made-up text or pricesSay: "Replace all placeholder text with my real content: [paste it]"
  • You hit the plan limitRun Prompt 5 to get a hand-off note. When the limit resets, start a new chat, paste the note and carry on.
  • Netlify doesn't show in ConnectorsUse option B on Go live: save the file and drag it into app.netlify.com/drop.
  • The WhatsApp button doesn't openThe number must be in international format with no + or spaces, like 2348012345678.
  • You can't share your screen on a MacOpen System Settings, then Privacy and Security, then Screen and System Audio Recording. Turn on Zoom, then restart Zoom.

Questions people asked

  • Can Claude work with an app my company already uses?Check claude.ai/customize/connectors first: many apps are already there. If the app has a website, Claude in Chrome can use it the way you do. If it's an internal app with an API, your IT team can add it as a custom connector. If it's none of those, Claude can't reach it yet.
  • Do I need a paid Claude plan?Not for the core method: Prompts 1 to 6 work on free plans, with limits. If you hit limits often, or want longer builds, connectors and Claude in Chrome, a paid plan is worth it.
  • Can I use this for client work?Yes. Be careful with what you paste in: don't put a client's private data or passwords into a chat unless they've agreed to it.
  • Can I share this kit?It's just for Cohort 01, which is why it asks for your reference and a code. Share your builds as widely as you like.
