PROMPT 1 · STRUCTUREIn ChatGPT

Fill in only the two brackets at the top. Don't answer the questions yourself: ChatGPT asks them one at a time, then writes the brief.

Portfolio page

Show 3 pieces of your work to a possible client.

4. Must have: My name and a one-line intro. 3 work cards, each with an image, a title, one line about it and a link. A contact button that opens an email or WhatsApp to me.
5. Not now: Blog, prices, testimonials, a contact form, more than 3 projects.
6. Content: Name: [your name]. Intro: [one line about what you do]. Work 1: [title, one line, image link, project link]. Work 2: [title, one line, image link, project link]. Work 3: [title, one line, image link, project link]. Contact: [your email or WhatsApp number].

WHEN YOU'RE READY

Your own domain

Buy the domain from any registrar (Namecheap, Cloudflare, Names.co.uk and so on). In Netlify, open your site, then Domain management, then Add a domain, and follow the DNS steps it shows. Netlify adds HTTPS for you. If the DNS step confuses you, screenshot it and ask Claude what to enter at your registrar.

Netlify's free plan has a monthly usage allowance. A small site like yours fits comfortably. If a site ever goes over, Netlify tells you, and you can upgrade or wait for the next month.

Pacing should feel like it's cut to music. Go crazy.

For [brand name]: [one line on what the brand does and who it's for].
Brand colours: [hex codes, or "take them from the logo"].

MOTION GRAPHICS LIBRARY

17 styles, ready to run

Each prompt sets a real style with numbers, not adjectives: colours, timing, easing, what to avoid. Swap the brackets for your brand and your message. Style list adapted from awesomeclaude.ai.

ADD TO ANY MOTION PROMPT

Five rules that lift every result

  • Numbers, not adjectives"#9040FF, 24 frames, 80px margins" beats "bold and modern".
  • A hook in the first secondThe first frame has to make someone stop scrolling.
  • Text needs timeAbout 0.3 seconds per word on screen, never less than 1 second, inside a 5% safe margin.
  • Hold the last frameThe final frame, usually the logo, stays still for about 1 second.
  • Say what to avoidEvery prompt below ends with an Avoid line. Keep it.
Before you build it, show me a storyboard: one line per shot with its timing. Wait for my OK.
End on a logo reveal: the logo forms from the shapes used in the piece, then holds for 1 second on [background colour].
Graphic and typeSwiss kinetic type · Shape morph · Vertical social captions
SWISS GRID AND KINETIC TYPE

Best for: brand identity, event openers, conference titles

Make a [length, e.g. 15]-second motion graphic in a Swiss grid and kinetic typography style for [brand or event].
Lines, in order: [3 to 5 short lines of text].
Format: [1920x1080 / 1080x1920 / 1080x1080], 60fps.
Style: a strict 12-column grid with 80px margins. Black, off-white, and one signal colour, [hex], on one element per shot only. One heavy grotesque typeface. Every move snaps on one easing curve, cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.2, 1). Shots change with hard wipes along the grid lines.
Avoid: fades, gradients, drop shadows, a second accent colour, centred layouts.
SHAPE MORPH AND LIQUID

Best for: music visuals, brand stories, moving between topics

Make a [length]-second shape morph animation for [brand] that tells this in 4 beats: [beat 1, beat 2, beat 3, beat 4].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: one continuous shot with no cuts. Each beat is a single bold shape that melts into the next over 18 to 30 frames. Hold each shape for at least 1 second so it reads. Palette: [3 hex colours], flat fills, smooth organic edges. The final shape becomes the logo.
Avoid: hard cuts, outlines, more than one shape moving at a time, text over the morphs.
VERTICAL SOCIAL CAPTIONS

Best for: Reels, Shorts, TikTok, founder clips

Make a [length, e.g. 20]-second vertical social video, 1080x1920, for [brand or person].
Script, as spoken: [paste the lines].
Style: big captions, 1 to 4 words at a time, timed to the script, kept inside the middle 80% of the frame so platform buttons don't cover them. One accent word per caption in [hex]. Background: [a colour, a slow gradient, or "my attached image"]. Captions pop in over 4 frames with a small overshoot.
Avoid: full sentences on screen, text in the bottom 20%, more than two colours of text.
Explain and sellFlat vector explainer · Data story · Dark tech keynote · Living UI
FLAT VECTOR EXPLAINER

Best for: product promos, onboarding, how it works

Make a [length, e.g. 30]-second flat vector explainer for [product].
The story in 4 steps: [the problem, what you do, how it works, the result].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: flat illustration with 4 to 5 colours, [hex codes], no gradients. Elements arrive with a small overshoot and settle within 12 frames. Groups of items stagger by 2 to 4 frames. Simple friendly characters with no facial detail.
Avoid: gradients, 3D, realistic textures, more than 7 words on screen at once.
DATA STORY

Best for: reports, impact numbers, investor updates

Make a [length]-second data story for [organisation] using only these numbers: [paste each figure with its source].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: one chart per screen. Bars and lines grow over 20 frames, then hold. Axis labels at least 28px. One highlight colour, [hex], for the number that matters, everything else in greys. Each screen ends on one plain sentence about what the number means.
Avoid: made-up numbers, 3D charts, pie charts with more than 4 slices, decorative icons.
DARK TECH KEYNOTE

Best for: product launches, feature reveals, AI and software

Make a [length, e.g. 15]-second launch film in a dark tech keynote style for [product].
The reveal: [the headline feature], and the one number that proves it: [the number].
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: near-black background, soft studio light, one accent colour, [hex]. Slow, confident camera moves of 30 to 45 frames. The key number appears huge, about 30% of the frame height. Clean sans serif, generous space.
Avoid: lens flares, fast cuts, busy backgrounds, more than one accent colour.
LIVING UI SCREENCAST

Best for: app tutorials, feature launches, showing your build

Rebuild the interface from the attached screenshots as real, crisp UI, not as pasted images.
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: a smooth cursor moves between targets over 20 to 30 frames, clicks with a soft ripple, and the camera zooms to 1.6x on each key detail, then eases back out. Short captions name each step.
Avoid: blurry screenshots, jumpy cursor moves, zooming more than once per step.

SINGLE LINE AND LIVE DRAWING

Best for: minimal ads, luxury brands, whiteboard explainers

Make a [length]-second animation drawn as one continuous line for [brand]. The line draws: [the scenes, in order], and finishes by drawing the logo.
Format: [size], 60fps.
Style: a single [colour] line with round caps on [background colour]. It draws at a steady pace, about 800 pixels a second at 1080p. The camera follows the pen tip and keeps it in the middle third of the frame.
Avoid: lifting the pen, fills, a second line colour, shading.
WATERCOLOUR AND INK

Best for: storytelling, travel, cultural and community brands

Format: [size], 24fps.
Style: everything painted, nothing vector. Ink lines draw first, then colour washes bloom in over 15 to 25 frames with soft wet edges. Warm paper texture throughout. Palette: [3 to 4 colours].
Avoid: pure white or pure black, hard digital edges, perfect circles.
RISOGRAPH AND HALFTONE PRINT

Best for: indie brands, event posters, zines

Format: [size], 12fps for a printed feel.
Style: two or three inks only, [e.g. fluorescent pink, blue, yellow], on off-white paper. Shading is halftone dots only. The ink layers sit 2 to 4 pixels out of line, like a real print.

DIRECTING

Talk like an art director

Your taste is the part AI can't supply. These are the phrases that turn fine into good.

VAGUE VS SPECIFICWith Prompt 3
  • Not "make it look better""Make the headline twice the size and the background off-white."
  • Not "make it pop""Make the Order button lime, full width, and fixed to the bottom."
  • Not "make it more modern""Use one font, more space between sections, and rounded corners on the cards."
  • Not "it's not working"Use Prompt 4, with a screenshot.

WHEN IT BREAKS

Fixes

Including the ones we hit together on Saturday.

  • Pictures from a website don't load in your buildClaude can only reach sites you allow. In Claude, open Settings, then Capabilities, and add the image site's domain to the allowed domains list (for property listings that was media.onthemarket.com). Then ask Claude to try again.
  • Your build answers like a chat, but you wanted a websiteSay: "Turn this into a website: one page that shows the results as cards, with a filter at the top and a link on each card. Keep the same data source."
  • Claude shows code but no pageCheck that code execution and file creation is on in Claude's settings. Then ask: "Show this as a page I can preview."
  • Claude changed things you didn't ask forSay: "Undo that. Go back to the previous version and change only [the one thing]."
  • Made-up text or pricesSay: "Replace all placeholder text with my real content: [paste it]"
  • You hit the plan limitRun Prompt 5 to get a hand-off note. When the limit resets, start a new chat, paste the note and carry on.
  • Netlify doesn't show in ConnectorsUse option B under Go live: save the file and drag it into app.netlify.com/drop.
  • The WhatsApp button doesn't openThe number must be in international format with no + or spaces, like 2348012345678.
  • You can't share your screen on a MacOpen System Settings, then Privacy and Security, then Screen and System Audio Recording. Turn on Zoom, then restart Zoom.
